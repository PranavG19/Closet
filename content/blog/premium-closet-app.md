[App Name] flips that. You grant photo access (or hand-pick specific photos if you'd rather), and the app finds the clothing photos already sitting in your camera roll. ## Your photos are checked on your device first

This matters, so we'll be plain about it.

Before anything leaves your phone, [App Name] runs an **on-device check** on your camera roll. It keeps the likely clothing and outfit photos and filters out the rest — intimate images, screenshots, photos that aren't clothing. **Only the clothing photos you approve are ever uploaded.** The private ones never leave your device.

We don't analyze your body. There's no covert scanning, no measuring, no biometric anything. Privacy here isn't a paragraph in a settings menu — it's the first step of the experience, working the way it should.

### A wardrobe you can actually browse

Your whole closet, laid out as clean cutouts you can scroll, and filter by category, color, or what's currently available. It's the closet you always meant to keep organized, without the folding.

### Weather-aware outfit suggestions

Ask [App Name] what to wear and it suggests outfits built from what you own and what's currently clean — biased toward today's weather, so you're not handed a linen dress on a cold, wet morning. Version one is smart and rule-based; it draws only from items that are actually available.

### A manual outfit builder

Want to compose looks yourself? Build an outfit slot by slot, save it, name it. Your saved outfits are yours to reuse — pull one up on a busy morning instead of starting from scratch.

### A laundry system that's kind about it

Every item is clean, in the wash, or unavailable (packed, at the cleaners, lent to a friend). Suggestions only ever pull from what's clean, so you're never handed an outfit you can't actually wear. The language stays neutral — laundry is normal life, not an error state.

### One-tap wear logging

Tap "I wore this" and [App Name] records it, and can nudge the item toward the wash. It takes a second, and over time it quietly builds a picture of what you actually reach for — the difference between the closet you *think* you have and the one you really wear.

### Color pairing help

When you're building or reviewing an outfit, [App Name] offers gentle color-pairing guidance — what goes together, what's a safe neutral, what makes a combination sing. It suggests. It never blocks you or scolds you. You wear what you want.

### A self-identified color palette (beta)

If you're curious about which colors flatter you, a short swatch quiz lets you tell us — you pick from swatches, we never guess from a camera. The result is an advisory palette that gently highlights aligning pieces in your suggestions. It's labeled beta, it's optional, and it never hides or forbids anything. Off-palette is still completely fine; it's your closet.

## FAQ

**Do I have to photograph my clothes one by one?**
No. That's the whole point. [App Name] finds the clothing photos already in your camera roll and digitizes them for you. You can also hand-pick specific photos if you'd prefer to control exactly what's included.

**What happens to my photos?**
They're checked on your device first. Only the clothing photos you approve are uploaded — intimate images, screenshots, and non-clothing photos are filtered out before anything leaves your phone.

**Does it scan my body or my skin tone?**
No. We never analyze your body. The color palette feature is a swatch quiz you fill out yourself — self-identified, never camera-detected — and it's purely advisory.

**Is there a free trial?**
No. The value is visible before you pay: you see your real digitized closet during onboarding, so you know what you're getting before you commit.

**Will it suggest clothes I can't wear right now?**
No. Suggestions only draw from items marked clean and available, so a dress in the wash won't show up as today's recommendation.

**Can I build my own outfits instead of using suggestions?**
Yes. The manual outfit builder lets you compose, name, and save your own looks to pull up whenever you like.
