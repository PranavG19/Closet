## Research — Garment harmony (color science)

### Hue-angle schemes map linearly to index-distance on the 12-bucket wheel; two established harmonious relationships (triadic, split-complementary) are currently misclassified as clash.
- **Science:** On the HSL/HSV hue circle, hue is an angle 0-360 deg. The standard color-scheme angles (Itten / mainstream design references) are: complementary ~180 deg, analogous adjacent (conventionally within ~30 deg), triadic = three hues 120 deg apart, split-complementary = a base plus the two hues flanking its complement (~150 deg / 210 deg, i.e. +/-30 deg off 180), square/tetradic = 90 deg. With 12 evenly spaced families, one index step = 360/12 = 30 deg, so index-distance d maps to d*30 deg: d=1 analogous(30), d=4 triadic(120), d=5 split-complementary(150), d=6 complementary(180). These even-spaced angular relations are exactly what a rotation-symmetric hue-distance lookup is built to express.
- **Maps to code:** harmony.ts verdictFor already computes the correct circular distance (0..6) but only names d=0/1/6; d=2,3,4,5 all fall through to 'clash'. Two of those are established harmonies: add d===4 -> a 'triadic' verdict and d===5 -> a 'split-complementary' verdict (extending HARMONY_VERDICTS and NOTE_BY_VERDICT, and slotting them into suggestionNote's RANK between complementary and clash). d=2 (60 deg) and d=3 (90 deg / tetradic-leg) remain genuinely weaker pairings and can stay 'clash'/soft. This is a pure, structural extension of the existing frozen table — no new inputs required.

### The code uses the additive HSL/RGB wheel geometry, whose complement of red is cyan — NOT the traditional painter's (RYB/Itten) red-green complement. The hex->family converter must therefore quantize the HSL/HSV hue angle, and the complement definition should be stated explicitly.
- **Science:** There are two long-standing color wheels. The RYB artist's wheel (Itten) pairs red with green as complements. The RGB/HSL wheel (standard HSL/HSV hue-angle math) pairs red(0 deg) with cyan(180 deg), green(120) with magenta(300), blue(240) with yellow(60). The 12 families in harmony.ts (red, orange, yellow, chartreuse, green, teal, cyan, azure, blue, violet, magenta, pink) are the evenly-spaced HSL hue names, and red(index 0) vs cyan(index 6) resolves to distance 6 = complementary — i.e. the code already commits to the HSL/additive wheel, not Itten's RYB.
- **Maps to code:** The known-missing F9 hex->family converter must compute HSL/HSV hue = atan2-based angle from #rrggbb and bucket by floor((hue+15)/30) mod 12 so families line up with the CHROMATIC index order harmony.ts assumes. A comment in harmony.ts should record that 'complementary' here means the HSL/additive complement (red<->cyan), so no one later 'fixes' it to the RYB red-green pairing and silently shifts every verdict by index.

### Value (lightness) is an independent axis: a shared hue relationship says nothing about value, and value contrast — not hue — is the primary driver of whether a combination reads. Monochromatic in particular needs value spread to be the 'layered tonal look' the note promises.
- **Science:** Every established color model is 3-dimensional and separates lightness from hue: Munsell (Hue / Value / Chroma), CIELAB (L* / C* / h), HSL/HSV (hue / saturation / lightness-or-value). Itten's seven contrasts list light-dark (value) contrast as its own contrast, distinct from hue contrast; value contrast governs figure-ground legibility. Consequence: two same-hue-family items (monochromatic verdict) with nearly equal L look flat/washed; the same two with a clear value gap read as an intentional tonal layering. Equally, a hue-'complementary' pair can still fail if their values are so close they lack the contrast that makes complementary pairings work.
- **Maps to code:** harmony() is family-token-only and cannot see this. Have the hex->family converter also emit L (HSL lightness or CIELAB L*) per garment, and let a refined harmony(a,b, la, lb) treat monochromatic as a strong 'tonal' verdict only when |la-lb| exceeds a small band, otherwise a softer 'flat' hint. suggestionNote's 'A tonal look — one colour, quietly layered' copy currently over-promises layering for two items of identical lightness; gating it on value spread makes the sentence honest without ever scolding.

### Chroma (saturation) is an independent axis: hue-harmonious colors can still clash on chroma, and two full-chroma complementaries placed adjacent produce simultaneous-contrast 'vibration' — visually harsh despite being 'correct' by hue.
- **Science:** Chroma/saturation is the third Munsell/CIELAB/HSL dimension. Itten lists contrast of saturation as its own contrast; combinations tend to cohere when chroma levels are related, and a lone max-chroma item among muted ones stands out (this is also why 'muted/earth-tone' palettes read as harmonious across hues that a pure hue rule would call discordant). Chevreul's law of simultaneous contrast and Albers (Interaction of Color) establish that adjacent complementaries at high chroma intensify each other at the boundary, producing after-image 'vibration' — a documented perceptual effect, not opinion.
- **Maps to code:** Emit chroma/saturation (HSV S or CIELAB C*) from the converter alongside the family. A refined harmony can then (a) soften a d=6 complementary from 'contrast' toward neutral-calm when BOTH items are high-chroma (avoid over-promising a pairing that may vibrate — and per the voice rule, never warn, just go quieter), and (b) NOT emit 'clash' for two mid-distance families when both are low-chroma, since muted palettes coexist. Because suggestionNote already renders clash as null (silent), the safe direction is to let chroma pull borderline pairs OUT of clash rather than into it.

### Neutrals pair broadly precisely because their chroma is ~0 — neutrality is a chroma threshold, not a fixed five-token list. Deriving it from chroma catches muted colors (dusty rose, sage) the hardcoded list forces into a chromatic family.
- **Science:** Black, white and gray are achromatic: chroma = 0, so they have no hue that can oppose another hue — they contribute value contrast without hue conflict, which is exactly why they combine with anything. Beige and navy are low-chroma (desaturated orange/yellow and desaturated dark blue). In Munsell/CIELAB terms, as C*->0 the hue coordinate becomes meaningless, so hue-based discord cannot arise. The code's 'neutral on either side -> neutral' rule is therefore sound, but it is really a low-chroma rule wearing a token-list costume.
- **Maps to code:** harmony.ts hardcodes NEUTRAL = [black,white,gray,beige,navy]. With chroma available from the converter, treat ANY family whose garment chroma is below a threshold as neutral-safe, regardless of its nominal hue bucket. That principled-ifies the neutral rule and correctly rescues a muted dusty-rose/sage/taupe (which today gets slotted into pink/green and can trigger a spurious 'clash') into the broadly-pairing neutral verdict. Keep the token list as the fallback when no chroma is known.

### A 12-family hue lookup is the RIGHT model for the hue-relationship layer, but it is TOO COARSE as the whole harmony verdict because it ignores value and chroma entirely and quantizes hue into 30-deg bins that are not perceptually uniform.
- **Science:** RIGHT: hue-scheme relationships are genuinely categorical, relational and rotation-symmetric, so a table keyed on sorted-pair hue-distance is a faithful, total, deterministic model of the angle rules — and symmetry-by-construction is correct because harmony is a relation, not directional. TOO COARSE: (1) it drops two of color's three dimensions, so it is wrong in both directions — a complementary pair can clash on value/chroma, and a nominal 'clash' pair that is both muted coexists fine; (2) 30-deg bins mean two hex colors ~31 deg apart can land two buckets apart (verdict 'clash') though they are perceptually near-analogous — quantization error is worst at bin boundaries; (3) equal index steps are NOT equal perceptual hue steps — Munsell renotation and CIELAB both show human hue spacing is non-uniform (e.g. the eye resolves far more distinct hues in blue-green than in the purples), so 'distance' is not perceptually linear.
- **Maps to code:** Keep harmony.ts's family table as the hue-relationship layer (it is cheap, robust to converter noise, and right for an advisory hint), but stop treating its verdict as the final word. Feed value+chroma from the hex->family converter into a thin post-adjust step (findings above). For boundary quantization, have the converter carry the raw hue angle so a near-boundary pair can be recognized as analogous even across a bucket edge. Do NOT try to encode perceptual non-uniformity in the family table — if that ever matters, move the distance computation to CIELAB hue-difference in the converter rather than complicating the frozen lookup.

### scorePalette's exact-token match is the coarsest layer of all; hue distance to the self-identified flattering set would recognize that a near-analogous color is still flattering — while staying strictly advisory and self-identified.
- **Science:** The swatch-quiz palette is a set of flattering hues. A garment one bucket (30 deg) off a chosen swatch is near-analogous to a flattering hue and, by the analogous-harmony principle, still reads as within the wearer's flattering range; an exact-string test scores it identically to its complement. Seasonal color analysis (the mainstream spring/summer/autumn/winter framework) organizes flattering ranges along value (light/deep), chroma (clear/muted/soft) and temperature (warm/cool) — i.e. neighborhoods in color space, not exact chips — which supports scoring by proximity rather than equality.
- **Maps to code:** palette.ts scorePalette currently returns withinPalette = hueSet.has(item.color) (exact token) with score 1/0. With the converter supplying each item's family (and ideally its hue angle + chroma), replace the binary with a graded score that decays with hue distance to the nearest flattering family (0 steps=1.0, 1 step high, larger steps lower) and treats low-chroma items as broadly compatible. This stays ADVISORY (still one annotation per id, nothing hidden/blocked/reordered-away, skin tone still self-identified) and is the piece that lets color finally inform suggestion.ts's ranking — the central gap — as a soft tie-breaker layered on top of the existing status=clean + thermal-warmth ranking, never a filter.

### Color temperature (warm/cool) is a real hue-based axis and must not be conflated with suggestion.ts's 'warmth', which is thermal insulation.
- **Science:** Warm hues (reds, oranges, yellows) vs cool hues (blues, greens, violets) is Itten's contrast of warm-cool, a hue-derived property. It is orthogonal to thermal warmth (how much a garment insulates). Seasonal color analysis uses color temperature as one of its three organizing axes.
- **Maps to code:** suggestion.ts's SuggestionItem.warmth is documented as a thermal insulation ordinal used for weather-monotone layering — it is NOT color temperature. If color temperature is ever added (e.g. from the hue angle: warm = families roughly red..yellow, cool = green..violet) it must be a separate field feeding the color/palette coherence layer, never merged into the thermal warmth sum, or the weather-monotonicity proof in suggestItems breaks.

**Flagged UNCERTAIN (not established science):**
- Specific numeric thresholds for value spread and chroma proximity that separate 'harmonious' from 'clash' are design heuristics, not established colorimetric constants — the AXES (value, chroma) are science; the cut points are tuning.
- Seasonal color analysis (spring/summer/autumn/winter) is a popular consulting framework, not peer-reviewed colorimetry. Its ORGANIZING AXES (value / chroma / temperature) are grounded in real color dimensions, but the specific per-season palette assignments and the four-season taxonomy itself are folk-system and should be treated as advisory convention, not fact.
- Whether a max-chroma complementary pair actually 'clashes' in a garment context is context-dependent — Itten's contrast of extension says the effect depends on the relative AREA of each color, which a family-pair lookup cannot see. The vibration/simultaneous-contrast effect at the boundary is established (Chevreul/Albers); calling it a wardrobe 'clash' is a judgment.
- The exact angular tolerance for 'analogous' (~30 deg is the common convention) and the tolerance bands for triadic/split-complementary are soft design conventions. The core scheme angles (180 / 120 / 90 / 150) are standard, but treating a hard 30-deg bin edge as the analogous/clash boundary is an approximation, not a perceptual law.
- I am confident equal HSL-hue steps are NOT perceptually uniform (Munsell renotation and CIELAB both show non-uniform hue spacing), but the precise perceptual correction (CIELAB delta-h, CIECAM02) is beyond what a 12-family lookup can or should encode; I have not verified any specific correction factor for this app.
- The claim that 'muted/low-chroma palettes coexist across hues that a pure hue rule would call discordant' is well-supported by contrast-of-saturation and by how earth-tone palettes work in practice, but I cannot cite a single hard numeric law defining the chroma level below which arbitrary hues are guaranteed to harmonize.


## Research — Skin-tone / undertone

### The rigorous skeleton of all personal-color analysis is the Munsell three-axis decomposition — Hue, Value, Chroma — not a warm/cool binary. Replace the binary token match with a graded fit across all three axes.
- **Science:** The Munsell system (standardized, used in ASTM D1535 and soil/color science) specifies any surface color by three perceptually-separated coordinates: Hue (the chromatic family), Value (lightness, 0 black -> 10 white), and Chroma (colorfulness/saturation from the neutral axis outward). This is established, textbook colorimetry. Every credible seasonal-color framework is, underneath its branding, just a partition of these same three axes: warm/cool = a hue-angle region, light/deep = a Value band, bright/soft (clear/muted) = a Chroma band. The three axes are near-orthogonal perceptually, which is exactly why a one-dimensional hue-token match throws away two-thirds of the signal.
- **Maps to code:** palette.ts scorePalette() currently reduces to `hueSet.has(item.color)` -> score in {0,1}. Convert each garment hex to (H, V, C): hue angle, lightness, chroma. Represent paletteProfile not as a Set of exact tokens but as a preferred REGION per axis (centroid + tolerance, or soft weights) captured by the swatch quiz. score becomes a continuous [0,1] affinity = weighted blend of circular-hue distance, value distance, and chroma distance, each passed through a smooth falloff. withinPalette stays a boolean but only as a soft label above a threshold — never a gate. Keep the existing invariant literally intact: one annotation per input item, every id preserved, nothing dropped.

### Warm vs cool undertone has a real but narrow colorimetric basis: a small directional shift in skin hue-angle driven by melanin vs hemoglobin. Map a self-identified undertone to a hue-angle PREFERENCE direction, never to a hard allow/deny hue set.
- **Science:** Skin color is produced by two dominant chromophores — melanin (eumelanin brown/black, pheomelanin reddish-yellow) and hemoglobin (oxygenated red, deoxygenated bluish) — this is established skin optics/dermatology. In CIELAB, the a* axis is red<->green and b* is yellow<->blue; skin occupies a comparatively narrow locus in the yellow-red quadrant (hue angles roughly in the 40-60 degree band, i.e. orange-ish). 'Warm' skin trends toward higher b* (golden/yellow), 'cool' toward a relatively pinker/rosier balance. The DIRECTION of the warm/cool distinction is real and colorimetrically describable; the magnitude of separation is small and the ranges overlap heavily. Itten's warm-cool contrast (Kunst der Farbe) formalizes the perceptual axis a garment color is judged on.
- **Maps to code:** The swatch quiz maps the user's self-ID to a target hue-angle bias (warm -> weight toward yellow-based hues ~30-90 degrees on the wheel; cool -> weight toward blue-based hues ~210-270; neutral -> flat weighting). In scorePalette this is a soft prior over the hue axis, combined with (not overriding) the garment's measured hue distance. Because the science says the separation is small, the hue weight should be gentle — a nudge, not a cliff — which also happens to be exactly what the 'advisory, never prescriptive' invariant requires.

### Whether a garment 'flatters' is a RELATIONAL, perceptual effect (simultaneous contrast) between the garment and the adjacent skin — not an intrinsic property of the color. This is the strongest scientific justification for framing everything as a hint about relationships, never a verdict on the person.
- **Science:** Chevreul's law of simultaneous contrast (De la loi du contraste simultane des couleurs, 1839) and Albers' Interaction of Color establish that a color's apparent hue, lightness, and saturation shift depending on its neighbors — a field induces its approximate complement in adjacent areas. This is a well-documented perceptual phenomenon. It means a garment near the face genuinely alters the APPARENT evenness/hue of skin (e.g., a cool field can reduce apparent sallowness), but the effect lives in the pairing, not in a fixed 'good/bad color.' Achromatic (Value) contrast is also perceptually dominant for form — differences in L* are more salient than hue differences.
- **Maps to code:** This validates keeping harmony.ts (garment-to-garment relations) as a first-class signal alongside palette fit, and validates suggestionNote.ts's design of speaking only about relationships ('these sit next to each other on the wheel') and going quiet on a clash. When wiring color into suggestion.ts (F5, the real recommender that today uses zero color), treat both palette fit AND inter-item harmony as graded, relational hints. The 'flattering' claim is never attached to the person — it stays a statement about how two garments sit together.

### The seasonal framework is real ONLY as the H/V/C decomposition plus warm-cool and value-contrast; the discrete season labels and 'you ARE a Winter' prescription are popular-but-soft. Model the palette as continuous preference weights, not a season identity.
- **Science:** ESTABLISHED: the four-season structure (Jackson, Color Me Beautiful, 1980), building on Itten's subjective-color work, Robert Dorr's earlier warm/cool Key-1/Key-2 system, and Suzanne Caygill, is a systematization of the three Munsell axes — warm/cool (hue), light/deep (value), clear/muted (chroma). The 12- and 16-sub-season refinements (e.g. Christine Scaman / 12 Blueprints) explicitly assign each season a primary + secondary axis characteristic. SOFT/UNVALIDATED: that a person objectively belongs to exactly one season, and that off-palette colors are measurably 'unflattering.' There is no peer-reviewed evidence for the prescriptive claim; what survives scrutiny is the dimensional vocabulary and the perceptual mechanisms underneath.
- **Maps to code:** Do NOT store a season label or derive a fixed allowed-hue set from one. Keep migration 0007's schema honest — palette_profile stores only a result, decoupled from derivation. Extend the stored 'hues' concept to soft per-axis preference weights (hue bias, value band, chroma band) rather than a discrete season enum. scorePalette then scores continuous fit against those weights, which is both more defensible science and structurally non-prescriptive: there is no bucket to be 'wrong' about.

### Hue-angle math is enough to build the missing hex->family converter (bucket the wheel into 12 x 30-degree bins; detect neutrals by chroma/lightness), but the GRADED fit score should compute distances in a perceptually-uniform space (CIELAB), not raw HSL, because HSL hue is perceptually non-uniform.
- **Science:** Standard HSL/HSV hue is a direct algebraic transform of RGB: hue = atan2-style angle around the RGB hexcone, 0-360 degrees. It is exact and cheap for BUCKETING into named families (each chromatic family ~30 degrees; neutrals fall out as low-saturation or near-black/near-white). But HSL is device-RGB and perceptually non-uniform — equal hue-degree steps are not equal perceived-hue steps, and its 'lightness' is not perceptual lightness. CIELAB (CIE 1976) was designed for approximate perceptual uniformity: L* is perceptual lightness, hue angle h_ab = atan2(b*, a*), chroma C*ab = sqrt(a*^2+b*^2), and color difference is a near-Euclidean distance. Munsell Value maps monotonically to L*. So distances that should mean 'how different do these look' belong in CIELAB, not HSL.
- **Maps to code:** Build the F9 hex->family converter (the known-missing piece) with plain HSL hue bins for the 12 CHROMATIC families in harmony.ts, plus neutral detection: very low saturation -> gray/black/white by lightness, and the special cases beige/navy by their hue+low-chroma+lightness signature. That unblocks harmony() feeding suggestion.ts. But for scorePalette's GRADED distance, convert hex -> CIELAB (via sRGB linearization -> XYZ -> Lab under D65) and compute hue/value/chroma distances there so 'fit' tracks perceived similarity. The family token stays the coarse layer; CIELAB is the fine layer.

### Camera detection of skin tone is a genuine quality risk, independent of privacy: an uncalibrated phone camera cannot recover true skin chromaticity, and the warm/cool signal is smaller than the measurement noise.
- **Science:** Established colorimetry: measured color = illuminant x surface-reflectance x sensor-response. Recovering a surface's true color requires a known illuminant and a characterized sensor. Phone cameras run automatic white balance and tone mapping — they deliberately move the recorded chromaticity to make the scene look neutral — so the same skin yields different pixel values under different lighting. Metamerism compounds this: different spectra match under one illuminant and diverge under another. Because the warm/cool skin separation is small in CIELAB (per the melanin/hemoglobin finding), it is easily swamped by white-balance and illuminant error. So a camera-derived undertone would be low-accuracy by construction, not just by policy.
- **Maps to code:** This is affirmative technical backing for the CLAUDE.md invariant that skin tone is self-identified via swatch quiz and NEVER camera-detected — it is a correctness argument, not only a privacy one. The swatch quiz sidesteps the entire illuminant/calibration problem: the user judges swatches against their own skin under their own lighting (their eyes ARE doing the color-constancy the camera can't), and reports a category. Keep derivation client-side and store only the result, as migration 0007 already does.

### Automated skin-tone classification is a representational/dignity minefield; self-identification plus advisory-only framing is the defensible design because it never makes a truth-claim about the user's body.
- **Science:** This is ethics/measurement rather than colorimetry, so stated carefully: it is well-documented that image datasets under-represent darker skin and that vision systems have historically performed worse on darker skin (a fairness/coverage problem). An automated classifier that tells a person what their skin 'is' encodes a notion of correct appearance and can misclassify in ways that are experienced as a dignity harm, and it turns an advisory aesthetic tool into an identity assertion about the body. Self-ID inverts this: the person is the authority on their own category, there is no biometric captured, and nothing can be leaked that never left the device.
- **Maps to code:** Frame the stored palette and every string derived from it as 'colours you told us you love,' never 'colours that suit you' / 'your season.' scorePalette must never emit a negative judgment about an item or the user: keep it symmetric with suggestionNote.ts, where a clash goes silent rather than scolding. The advisory contract in palette.ts's header (never remove, hide, reorder-away, or block) is the ethical guarantee made structural — preserve it exactly when going graded.

### The graded score must feed suggestion.ts (F5) only as a SOFT secondary preference that never overrides the proven wearability and warmth-monotone guarantees, and never filters an item. This closes the gap without becoming prescriptive.
- **Science:** This is a design consequence of the above, not a color-science claim. The defensible-advisory posture requires: no hard cutoff (a low fit means 'less emphasized,' never hidden), monotone/continuous scoring (no cliff where a garment suddenly becomes 'wrong'), and preservation of every item. Relational hints (harmony) and preference hints (palette fit) are combined additively as a gentle re-rank among options that are ALREADY wearable.
- **Maps to code:** suggestion.ts today ranks on status=='clean' + warmth + category and uses zero color. Keep that primary ordering — the warmth-monotone proof and the 'never suggest an unwearable garment' invariant depend on it. Add color as a graded TIE-BREAKER / bonus applied AFTER the wearability filter and warmth sort: among clean, equally-warm candidates, prefer higher blended color affinity = f(scorePalette fit, inter-item harmony via harmony() over the hex->family tokens). Color can reorder near-ties and drive the suggestionNote copy; it must never exclude a clean item, never lower recommended warmth, and never turn a hint into a filter.

**Flagged UNCERTAIN (not established science):**
- The exact hue-angle boundary separating 'warm' from 'cool' skin: the distinction is directionally real (higher b*/golden vs pinker balance) but the ranges overlap heavily and there is no crisp colorimetric threshold. Treat warm/cool as a soft bias, not a partition.
- Whether any given seasonal palette is objectively 'more flattering' than another for a person: NOT established. No peer-reviewed support. Only the underlying H/V/C axes and simultaneous/value-contrast mechanisms are solid; the prescriptive payoff is popular-but-soft.
- The 12- and 16-sub-season refinements (Bright Winter, Soft Autumn, etc.): a coherent systematization of the three axes but empirically unvalidated as flattery predictions — popular, not scientific.
- 'Match garment value-contrast to the person's own hair/skin/eye contrast for flattery': the perceptual salience of Value (L*) contrast IS established, but the flattery heuristic built on it is a personal-color convention, not a proven result.
- The precise chromaticity centroids of 'warm' vs 'cool' skin: the a*/b* DIRECTION is defensible; specific numeric centroids/tolerances vary by population and lighting and should be treated as tunable, not fixed constants.
- OKLab as strictly superior to CIELAB for skin-fit distances: OKLab (Ottosson, 2020) is plausibly better for perceptual uniformity but is recent and less 'textbook-established' than CIELAB; I recommend CIELAB as the confident choice and flag OKLab as a reasonable-but-newer option, not settled science.
- The specific hex->family bin edges and neutral-detection thresholds (saturation/lightness cutoffs for gray/black/white/beige/navy): HSL bucketing is standard, but the exact cutoffs are engineering choices needing empirical tuning against the garment corpus, not derivable from first principles.


## Research — Explainability / voice

### An explanation must state the ACTUAL ranking reason, or be framed as a neutral observation — never a plausible-sounding story the model didn't use. This is the honesty core of the whole feature.
- **Science:** Recommender-explainability research (Tintarev & Masthoff's explanation-aims taxonomy: transparency, scrutability, trust, effectiveness) distinguishes an EXPLANATION (faithful to the model's actual reasoning) from a JUSTIFICATION (a post-hoc plausible rationale). A justification dressed as an explanation destroys the trust it's meant to build once the user notices the mismatch.
- **Maps to code:** suggestion.ts (F5) ranks on status=clean + warmth + category and reads ZERO color. Therefore suggestionNote.ts is NOT explaining why the outfit was chosen — it is describing a property of the set after the fact. The current copy is correctly descriptive/observational ('A tonal look — one colour, quietly layered.') rather than causal, so it stays honest despite the gap. RULE: keep suggestionNote phrasing observational ('this is…', 'these sit…') and never causal ('we picked this because…') UNTIL harmony/palette actually feed F5's ranking. Only when F9's hex->family output is wired into suggestion.ts does causal phrasing ('paired for the tonal match') become true.

### Harmony is a property of a RELATIONSHIP between colors, never of one garment — so an explanation names the pair, and a lone color yields no note.
- **Science:** Albers, Interaction of Color: color is the most relative medium in art; a color's effect is determined by its neighbors. Itten's seven contrasts (contrast of hue, complementary contrast, etc.) are all defined BETWEEN colors, not on a single swatch. There is no such thing as the 'harmony' of one color in isolation.
- **Maps to code:** suggestionNote.ts already computes over PAIRS and takes the worst pair (the two least-harmonious garments), which is the correct relational model — one safe pair should not vouch for a set that clashes elsewhere. Keep that. It also correctly returns null when families.length < 2 (a single known color is not a pairing). This is not just an edge case; it is the science: with one color there is no relation to describe.

### Map the hue-angle verdict to ONE plain sensory line — the friend names the effect, not the geometry. No jargon ('complementary', 'analogous', 'palette').
- **Science:** Standard HSL/HSV and artist-wheel hue-angle math: same hue = monochromatic (0°), adjacent = analogous (~30°), opposite = complementary (180°). These are technical labels for perceptual effects — a tonal/quiet look, an easy blend, a high-contrast pairing. Communication research (Grice's maxim of manner: avoid obscurity) says translate the term into the felt effect.
- **Maps to code:** NOTE_BY_VERDICT in suggestionNote.ts already does this translation well: 'monochromatic' -> 'A tonal look', 'analogous' -> 'the blend is easy'. One refinement: the complementary line ('Opposite hues — this one has some contrast to it.') still leaks the wheel metaphor ('opposite hues'); a friend would say 'These play off each other — a bit of contrast, on purpose.' The verdict->copy Record being closed over HarmonyVerdict (compile error on a missing sentence) is the right structure; keep clash deliberately absent.

### ADVISORY vs PRESCRIPTIVE is autonomy-supportive vs controlling language. Advisory describes/offers and leaves her the choice; prescriptive commands, evaluates HER choice, or implies a rule she's breaking.
- **Science:** Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan): autonomy-supportive language ('you might…', 'if you like…', descriptive framing) preserves felt choice and increases uptake; controlling language ('you should', 'must', 'don't') undermines it. Brehm's psychological reactance: a perceived threat to freedom provokes push-back and re-assertion of the freedom — the mechanism behind 'never bossy.' Test for prescriptive copy: does it contain a second-person imperative or an evaluative verdict on her outfit?
- **Maps to code:** The NOTE_BY_VERDICT sentences pass the test — no imperative, no verdict on her. Enforce this as the copy contract for every future note across suggestionNote.ts AND any palette-facing copy: no 'wear/avoid/don't/should', no 'clashes/wrong/off'. palette.ts's withinPalette=false must render as absence of a positive highlight, never as a negative label. Concretely in SuggestionsScreen.tsx the note renders as a gentle left-border 'highlight' strip (advisory affordance) — correct; it must never become a red/state color or a badge.

### Silence is a first-class output. Emit a note ONLY when it is simultaneously TRUE, INFORMATIVE, and non-face-threatening; otherwise return null. This is a rule, not a fallback.
- **Science:** Grice's maxim of quality (do not say what you lack evidence for) and quantity (don't say more than is informative) — filler text violates both. Brown & Levinson politeness theory: pointing out a clash is a face-threatening act; when you cannot mitigate it, the polite move is to not perform it. Minimal-footprint explanation in RecSys: an explanation that adds no decision value should be suppressed.
- **Maps to code:** suggestionNote.ts already encodes the exact three-part rule: null on <2 known colors (not informative — nothing to compare), and null on clash (cannot be said without scolding — fails non-face-threatening). Make this the stated contract: NoteGate = true AND informative AND kind; fail any -> null. The UI must treat null as 'render nothing' (SuggestionsScreen.tsx does: `note !== null &&`). Never introduce a default/placeholder sentence — the prior hardcoded 'This pairs beautifully with your neutrals.' was exactly the Grice-quality violation this replaced.

### When the app does NOT act on her feedback, attribute the outcome to an external CONSTRAINT and keep her in control — never defend the algorithm and never imply she was wrong.
- **Science:** Attribution theory + politeness theory: locating the cause in the situation ('it's in the wash', 'it's freezing out') rather than in her judgment avoids a face threat and avoids reactance (Brehm). Autonomy support (SDT) says close with agency ('want it anyway? tap to add'), which converts a refusal into a choice. Defending the model ('our algorithm decided…') is the controlling frame that provokes push-back.
- **Maps to code:** No code path today (this is the GAP — F5 ignores feedback entirely). When built, the copy pattern per conflict class: (a) she rejects a suggestion -> say nothing, silently re-suggest a different set; a rejection is data, not a debate. (b) feedback conflicts with availability/weather -> attribute to the fact: 'That one's in the wash right now — here's a close stand-in.' / 'It's cold for that today — want it anyway?' (c) feedback conflicts with HER OWN stated palette (palette.ts input) -> defer to her, because it is her self-identified input, not a law: 'This sits outside the palette you picked — still want to feature it?' Always: acknowledge -> attribute to external fact -> offer agency. Never: justify F5's ranking.

### The palette/skin-tone signal is advisory by scientific necessity, not just by product taste — so it can annotate but must never filter, and off-palette must be silence, not a warning.
- **Science:** Seasonal color analysis (Color Me Beautiful lineage) is a popular styling FRAMEWORK, not established color science — its category boundaries are heuristic and its 'you can't wear X' form is prescriptive by origin, exactly the voice docs/03 forbids. Combined with the CLAUDE.md invariant that skin tone is self-identified (never camera-detected), the only honest posture is advisory: a hint she can ignore.
- **Maps to code:** palette.ts scorePalette() is already advisory-by-construction (one annotation per item, preserves every id, drops/blocks nothing). Preserve that. In copy: withinPalette=true -> a gentle positive highlight ('one of your colours'); withinPalette=false -> NO copy at all (absence of highlight), never 'off-palette'/'not your colour'. This mirrors the suggestionNote silence rule: the negative case is expressed by the absence of the positive, never by a negative label.

### Hedge every color claim because the model sees only hue-family, not full color. Avoid superlatives in both directions ('perfect', 'clashes').
- **Science:** Munsell decomposes color into three independent dimensions — hue, value (lightness), chroma (saturation). Two garments in the same hue family can still read as discordant on value/chroma (e.g., a pale dusty blue vs a saturated royal blue). A hue-family-only model cannot see that, so any confident 'these match / these clash' claim overstates the model's evidence.
- **Maps to code:** harmony.ts operates on 12 hue tokens + 5 neutrals and the (known-missing) hex->family converter will collapse value and chroma entirely. So the copy must stay hedged: 'has some contrast to it' (present) is right; 'these are perfect together' / 'these clash' would claim more than the model knows. Keep NOTE_BY_VERDICT's soft register; when the hex->family converter lands, treat low-chroma hexes as neutral-family (near-achromatic) so muted tones correctly route to the safe 'neutral' verdict rather than a false chromatic verdict.

**Flagged UNCERTAIN (not established science):**
- Seasonal color analysis (spring/summer/autumn/winter 'seasons') is a widely-published styling framework, NOT established color science — its category boundaries and its 'flattering/unflattering' claims are heuristic and contested. Safe to use only as an advisory, self-identified hint; do not present it as fact.
- harmony.ts models the wheel as 12 EVENLY spaced hues and defines complementary as index+6 (exactly 180° on that wheel). Perceptual/CIE and Munsell hue spacing is NOT uniform, and 'complementary' pairs differ between the artist/RYB wheel, the RGB/light wheel, and perceptual opponent axes. The 12-even-hue wheel is a defensible modeling convention, but 'these two are complementary' is a property of THIS model's wheel, not a colorimetric truth. Copy should not over-claim it.
- Whether complementary/high-contrast pairings are 'pleasing' is aesthetic preference, not established fact. Itten codified the contrasts; Albers explicitly argued AGAINST fixed universal harmony rules. So harmony verdicts are useful descriptive labels, not a guarantee an outfit looks good — another reason the copy must describe ('has contrast') rather than endorse ('looks great').
- Classifying navy and beige as 'neutrals' is a fashion/styling convention, not colorimetry — both carry real hue and chroma (unlike true Munsell neutrals, which are achromatic: black/white/gray). The neutral-safe rule in harmony.ts is a reasonable product simplification, but it is a convention, not a color-science law.
- I did not find an existing feedback/rejection data path in the codebase (grep for reject/dismiss/feedback in packages/ only hit DB error handling and RLS tests). The 'why we didn't act on feedback' copy patterns are therefore recommendations for a not-yet-built seam, grounded in behavioral-design principles, not descriptions of current code.
